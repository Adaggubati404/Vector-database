This note is about a day of hiking in the hills, far from any kitchen.

Lace your boots before you step onto the trail. A hiking trail with steady elevation is easier to follow in the morning, when the ridge is cool. Carry water, a paper map, and a jacket in case the wind picks up above the tree line. Tell someone which trailhead you left from.

The best hiking routes gain elevation gradually. Watch the blazes, keep your boots dry at the stream crossing, and turn around if the ridge disappears into cloud. Reaching the summit marker is the point of the hike. Loose stone on a steep trail will shift under tired boots, so place each step on purpose.

After hiking, check your boots for mud and mark the trail you finished on the map. Elevation you climbed today is the number worth writing down. A short hiking loop still counts when the elevation is honest and the boots come home muddy.
